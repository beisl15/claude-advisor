## 1. Tese de investimento

Vale is the world's largest iron ore producer and Brazil's largest exporter, with operations positioned at the bottom of the global cost curve. The investment thesis is a combination of stable dividend income (6-10% yield through the cycle), Chinese steel demand as the structural driver, and a management team that has rebuilt credibility and capital discipline following the Mariana and Brumadinho tailings dam disasters.

## 2. Fosso economico (moat)

Vale's moat is asset quality and geography. The Carajás iron ore mines in Pará state contain some of the highest-grade iron ore deposits in the world (65-67% Fe content vs. 58-62% for Australian peers), commanding a premium in Chinese steel markets due to lower impurity content. The port and logistics infrastructure (rail network, Tubarão and Ponta Madeira port terminals) represents billions in sunk capital that cannot be replicated at current costs. The nickel division (from the INCO acquisition) provides exposure to EV battery demand, though this remains a relatively small contributor to group earnings. Geographic proximity to Asia via Cape Verde shipping routes provides freight advantages versus Brazilian competitors.

## 3. Narrativa do negocio

Vale's financial narrative is defined by iron ore prices (driven by Chinese steel demand), production volumes (constrained by operational licenses after the tailings dam disasters), and the gradual de-rating as ESG concerns reduce the buyer universe. The 2023-2024 period saw production normalization after dam-related restrictions — volumes are recovering to 320-330Mt annually. Chinese property sector weakness is the bear case: Chinese real estate and infrastructure account for approximately 60% of global steel demand, and the current real estate correction has pressured iron ore prices from $150/t peaks to $90-110/t. The copper division (Sossego, Salobo) provides meaningful diversification into a structurally bullish commodity.

## 4. Scuttlebutt

Operational audits post-Brumadinho confirm Vale has invested $2B+ in tailings dam remediation and decommissioning — the ESG liability is being actively managed. China steel production data (monthly releases) is the most important leading indicator for Vale's operational metrics. Brazilian environmental licensing has improved as Vale's safety record has normalized — production license risk is reduced but not eliminated. The nickel business has faced structural pressure from Indonesian supply growth (lower-cost HPAL processing), reducing nickel's contribution to portfolio earnings. The base metals division is the most interesting long-term optionality due to the copper/nickel EV content growth thesis.

## 5. Alocacao de capital

Vale returned to shareholder capital allocation discipline post-2022 under the new management team. The dividend policy (minimum $2.4B per year base dividend, plus buybacks to maintain leverage targets) is transparent and executable. Share buybacks have been aggressive — share count has declined approximately 20% from peak levels, concentrating earnings per share for remaining shareholders. Net debt of $5-6B is conservative for a company of Vale's asset quality and cash generation, providing room for opportunistic capital deployment (copper M&A, base metals expansion) without balance sheet risk. ROIC has normalized to 15-20% — below the historical peak but reasonable given the commodity cycle position.

## 6. Margem de seguranca

Vale's margin of safety is provided by its position on the global iron ore cost curve — at $65/t iron ore, Vale still generates positive FCF while high-cost producers exit. At current prices ($90-110/t), Vale generates significant earnings and dividend capacity. The Mariana/Brumadinho liability settlement (Samarco JV and Brumadinho legal claims) has been provisioned but remains an ongoing cash outflow. The single largest risk is a sharp Chinese real estate recovery failure causing iron ore prices to fall below $80/t — this would compress dividends but not threaten solvency given Vale's cost position. At 4-7x forward EV/EBITDA, Vale is cheap relative to global mining peers on an asset-quality-adjusted basis.
